# Three ways to stop comfort complaints before they start

If you run a building, you know the email. "It's freezing in here." Or the phone call. "Can someone look at the second floor? It's like a sauna."

Comfort complaints are part of the job. But they don't have to run your day. Most of them come from a few simple causes. Fix those, and the complaints slow down.

Here are three ways to get ahead of them.

## 1. Know before they do

Most complaints arrive after a room has been uncomfortable for a while. By then, people are annoyed. And you're playing catch-up.

The fix is to see problems early. Put sensors where people actually sit, not just near the thermostat. Watch the readings. Set alerts for when a room drifts out of range.

- Put sensors near desks, not doors
- Set an alert range for each zone
- Check the trend, not just the number

With Lumen Hub, you get an alert when a zone drifts. You can often fix it before anyone notices. That's the best kind of complaint: the one that never happens.

## 2. Set ranges, not rules

Here's a pattern you've probably seen. One person feels cold. They turn the heat way up. Then they leave. The next person walks into an oven.

You can't make everyone feel the same. But you can stop the extremes. Give each zone a comfort range. Let people adjust inside it. Don't let them go past it.

People still feel in control. The room stays reasonable. And your equipment doesn't work overtime.

Lumen Hub lets you set a range for every zone. People can tap the screen to warm up or cool down. They just can't push it too far.

## 3. Match the schedule to real life

Many buildings run on a schedule someone set years ago. The building opens at 7. The heat comes on at 5. Nobody remembers why.

Take an hour this month to check your schedules against how people really use the space. Is that meeting room empty on Fridays? Does the front office open late on Mondays?

- Walk the building at opening time
- Ask your team when rooms are busy
- Change one schedule at a time

Small changes add up. You keep people comfortable when they're there. You save energy when they're not.

## A note on air quality

Comfort isn't only about temperature. A stuffy room feels bad even when the temperature is fine. People get tired. They get headaches. They complain, even if they can't say why.

Watch your air quality readings, especially in meeting rooms. If a room gets stuffy every afternoon, it may need more fresh air during busy hours.

## Start small

You don't need a big project to cut complaints. Pick one floor. Add sensors. Set ranges. Check the schedules. See what changes in a month.

Then do the next floor.
